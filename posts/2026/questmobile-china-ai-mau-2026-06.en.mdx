Doubao hit 382 million monthly active users in China in June 2026.

QuestMobile's ranking that month: Doubao (ByteDance) 382M, Qwen (Alibaba) 167M, DeepSeek 129M. Media often rounds DeepSeek to 130M. The QuestMobile figure is 129M.

In China the leaderboard is not the Western one. Distribution apps beat the model the West argues about.

## What the ranking actually says

Year on year, again citing the same QuestMobile report (Phoenix Tech): Doubao +172.1%, Qwen +5792.9%, DeepSeek -20.3%. AI-native apps as a group were about 499 million monthly active users.

Source: QuestMobile, 2026 AI Application Market Half-Year Report, published July 14, 2026.

The West argues about the model. This ranking is not that argument.

Doubao is a distribution app. It sits where users already are. Qwen, in this ranking, is also an app with distribution behind it. DeepSeek is the name Western coverage keeps returning to, and on this chart it is third, and down year on year.

In China, the app that owns distribution is what shows up at the top of the MAU list.

## What I saw in a Shenzhen finance room

A few days earlier I was at Kimi's finance session at Alibaba Houhai in Shenzhen. Banks, funds, brokers, and wealth managers were on stage.

My take: real enterprise rollout is still far off. Putting a model on order flow or on customer data is hard in every country.

China's privacy bar is not the same as elsewhere. Personal data is already scattered across many systems. Kimi can also be self-hosted from open weights. That combination makes it easier for a large model to get in.

China will not wait for a perfect rollout before it uses AI to change the experience. Risk-control systems and support pages already run on models.

The scorecard I would watch is simple: how many jobs get cut early, and how fast AI lifts revenue.

## The same point, two rooms

One chart says consumer attention in China already accrued to distribution apps, not to the model the West keeps debating.

One room says enterprise finance is still early, and that the work is already happening anyway, in risk control and support, before anyone would call the rollout done.

China is not waiting for the Western conversation to settle. That is true of the leaderboard. It is also true of how these tools get wired into real workflows.
